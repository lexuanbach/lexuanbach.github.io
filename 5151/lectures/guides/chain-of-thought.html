Chain-of-Thought Prompting Elicits Reasoning in Large Language Models

The single prompting trick underneath every reasoning technique in Week 2 — and the paper that first showed writing out intermediate steps is not decoration, it changes what a large model can actually solve.

Read the paper ↗ Wei, Wang, Schuurmans, Bosma, Ichter, Xia, Chi, Le & Zhou · NeurIPS 2022 ← Back to Reasoning & Planning
01 · In plain terms

Show your work — it turns out the model needs to too

Ask a model a multi-step math word problem and demand only the final number, and it often gets it wrong. Ask the same model to write out its reasoning step by step before the answer, and — with no retraining, no extra data, nothing but a change in the prompt's few-shot examples — accuracy jumps dramatically. That is the entire finding. It sounds almost too simple to be a landmark paper, but the mechanism matters: an autoregressive model generates each token conditioned on everything before it, so forcing intermediate reasoning tokens into the output literally gives the model more computation, spent on the problem, before it commits to an answer. Skip straight to the answer, and that computation never happens.

02 · How it works

Few-shot exemplars, no fine-tuning

Chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting is purely a prompt-construction technique: instead of few-shot exemplars that show (question, answer) pairs, you show (question, reasoning steps, answer) triples. The model, conditioned on this pattern, generates its own reasoning steps for the new question before answering. No gradient update happens anywhere.

The effect is emergent — it appears reliably only past a certain model scale. Wei et al. showed CoT barely helps (and sometimes hurts) small models, but produces large, consistent gains once models are large enough (roughly 100B+ parameters at the time). This scale dependence is itself an important critical point: CoT is not a universal free lunch you can assume works for any model size, including many of today's smaller open-weight models run locally for a course project.

GSM8K math word problems, PaLM 540B, 8-shot prompting. Wei et al., NeurIPS 2022.
Key result On GSM8K, 8-shot chain-of-thought prompting with PaLM 540B reached ≈57% accuracy (≈58% combined with an external calculator), versus ≈18% for standard prompting — beating even a fine-tuned GPT-3 with a trained verifier, with zero fine-tuning.
Worked exampleThe same question, two prompting styles

Q: A juggler can juggle 16 balls. Half of the balls are golf balls, and half of the golf balls are blue. How many blue golf balls are there?

Standard promptingA: 8. (wrong — skipped the second "half") Chain-of-thoughtThe juggler has 16 balls. Half are golf balls, so there are 16 / 2 = 8 golf balls. Half of the golf balls are blue, so there are 8 / 2 = 4 blue golf balls. Answer4. (correct)

Nothing about the model changed between these two runs — only whether the prompt asked it to externalize the two-step division before answering.

04 · For your talk & project

What this buys you

  • If you present S1-1 Advanced Reasoning & Planning or S1-6 Self-improvement & Reflection: CoT is the common ancestor of both topics — ReAct interleaves it with actions, Self-Consistency samples many CoT chains and votes, ToT/GoT search over CoT-style intermediate steps. Naming this lineage explicitly is exactly what "explained at the mechanism level" means in the required talk structure.
  • The emergence-at-scale finding is a genuine limitation worth raising in Q&A: does your project's chosen model (especially if it's a smaller local model) actually benefit from CoT prompting, or do you need to verify that empirically rather than assume it?
  • For your project's prompting strategy: if your agent's reasoning quality is weak, CoT-style prompt restructuring is often a cheaper fix to try before reaching for a more expensive technique like ToT.
