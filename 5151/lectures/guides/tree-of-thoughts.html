Tree of Thoughts: Deliberate Problem Solving with Large Language Models

Chain-of-thought commits to one line of reasoning and never looks back. Tree of Thoughts explores several, evaluates them, and backtracks from dead ends — turning generation into search.

Read the paper ↗ Yao, Yu, Zhao, Shafran, Griffiths, Cao & Narasimhan · NeurIPS 2023 ← Back to Reasoning & Planning
01 · In plain terms

Explore several ideas at once, keep the promising ones

Chain-of-thought produces one linear sequence of reasoning steps; if an early step is wrong, everything downstream inherits the mistake with no way back. Tree of Thoughts (ToT) instead treats each intermediate reasoning step as a node in a tree: at each point, the model generates several candidate next thoughts, a separate evaluation step scores how promising each one looks, and a classic search algorithm (breadth-first or depth-first search) decides which branches to keep expanding and which to abandon. It's the difference between writing down the first idea that comes to mind and sketching three approaches on scratch paper before committing to one.

02 · How it works

Generate, evaluate, search — and the evaluator is the bottleneck

ToT needs four things defined for any task: how to decompose the problem into thought steps, how to generate candidate thoughts at each step (sample several, or propose them sequentially), how to evaluate a partial solution's promise (the model judges its own intermediate states — "is this on track?"), and which search algorithm to run over the resulting tree (BFS keeps the best-k states at each level; DFS goes deep on one branch and backtracks on failure). The evaluation step is the one to scrutinize hardest: it's the same model doing the judging, with no ground truth to check against, so ToT's gains are capped by how good the model actually is at recognizing its own good ideas — a weak evaluator makes the whole search directionless.

Breadth-first search over a tree of thoughts, with pruning. Yao et al., NeurIPS 2023.
Key result On the "Game of 24" arithmetic puzzle, GPT-4 with chain-of-thought prompting alone solved 4% of instances; GPT-4 with Tree of Thoughts solved 74% — the same base model, restructured only by search.
Worked exampleGame of 24 — why chain-of-thought gets stuck

Task: using the numbers 4, 9, 10, 13 exactly once each, reach 24 with +, −, ×, ÷.

CoT (one line)13 − 9 = 4, then 4 × 4 = 16, then 16 + 10 = 26. Wrong, and no way back once committed. ToT branch A10 − 4 = 6 → evaluator: "6, 9, 13 left, plausible" → keep exploring. ToT branch B13 + 9 = 22 → evaluator: "22, 4, 10 left, hard to reach 24 exactly" → prune. ToT continues on A13 − 9 = 4, then 10 − 4 = 6, then 6 × 4 = 24. Solution found by backtracking from a pruned path.
04 · For your talk & project

What this buys you

  • If you present S1-1: use ToT as the pivot between "reasoning" and "planning" — Graph of Thoughts generalizes the tree to a graph (thoughts can merge, not just branch), and LATS adds real environment feedback into the same search skeleton. Say what each one changes relative to this baseline.
  • The evaluator-is-the-bottleneck point is the strongest critical angle available: ask what happens when the model's self-evaluation is unreliable — does the search still help, or does it just spend more tokens confidently pruning the right branch?
  • For your project: ToT-style search is expensive (many more model calls per decision) — if you use it, your evaluation (R5) needs to report that cost explicitly, per the "AI Agents That Matter" critique.
