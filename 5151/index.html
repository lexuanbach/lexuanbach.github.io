Advanced Agentic Artificial Intelligence

Course motto: learning through working. Every idea in this course — the agent loop, self-attention, memory, evaluation — is something you run, break, and rebuild, not just read about.

A research seminar on the architectures, memory, tools and multi-agent systems that turn large language models into autonomous agents — paired with a group project that builds, benchmarks and red-teams a complete agentic AI system. No midterm, no final exam: the course is assessed entirely through two seminars and a project.

01 · What the course covers

From LLM to autonomous agent

Ten threads run through the semester: agent research foundations, reasoning & planning (ReAct, Reflexion, Tree/Graph-of-Thoughts), tool use & MCP, agent memory, coding agents, multi-agent systems, agent security & safety, reinforcement learning for agents, production & governance, and research/presentation/critique skills.

Coming from CO3131? CO3131 (Next-gen Software Engineering) teaches you to apply coding agents and MCP inside the software process. CO5151 goes underneath that: the primary research literature on reasoning, memory, multi-agent orchestration, and — critically — formal threat modeling and defenses for agent security, not just recognizing that prompt injection exists. Prior hands-on experience is an asset for the project; it is not a substitute for reading the papers.
02 · Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, you can

L.O.1 · Knowledge

Analyze & critique research

  • Synthesize and critique the main research directions in agentic AI from primary literature
  • Compare assumptions, strengths, and limits of competing agent techniques and architectures
L.O.2 · Skills

Design & build agents

  • Design agent/multi-agent architectures integrating memory, tools (MCP), and orchestration
  • Implement, experiment with, and benchmark an agent system on a real task
L.O.3 · Skills

Assess security & safety

  • Analyze and model agent-specific threats — prompt injection, tool misuse, memory poisoning
  • Propose defenses: guardrails, permission control, monitoring, and safety evaluation
L.O.4 · Autonomy

Research & lead

  • Deliver a thematic seminar from the literature; critique and defend technical arguments
  • Lead a project team; practice research ethics and responsible AI use
03 · How the final grade is calculated

Two seminars, one project — no exam

Assessment is entirely continuous: there is no midterm and no final exam. Every point comes from delivering and defending a seminar, or from designing, building and defending a working agentic AI system.

04 · Course structure

Four phases across 13 real sessions

The project runs in parallel with every phase — a proposal in Week 2, a checkpoint in Week 7, and the defense in Weeks 12–13.

Why the site lists 13 sessions The syllabus describes 15 sessions, but the registered HK261 timetable provides 13 class meetings: Wednesdays, 18:00–20:29, in B4-305, with no class on 14 October during the university midterm-exam week. To fit the registered calendar, the first two foundation lectures share Week 1, and the Seminar 2 wrap-up is included in Week 11. Seminar presentation time and both project-defense sessions remain unchanged.
